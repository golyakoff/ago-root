# Amend `adr/0027`: tenancy and identity unify, domains stay apart

- **Stage**: 22
- **Status**: done (2026-09-03) - `adr/0093`
- **Found**: 2026-09-03, deciding how AGO Calendar is sold. Every other item in Stage 22 cites this
  one, so it lands first or the rest has no anchor.

## What changed since `adr/0027`

Nothing about the code. Two things about the situation.

**The purpose.** `adr/0027`'s closing argument is that separation *"reinforces the platform claim… a
harder and more honest proof that the platform/product boundary holds."* That is an argument about
proving an architecture. The project is now launching a product to a paying client, and the person
paying experiences the seam as a defect.

**A third product shipped and did it differently.** `19-03` (done 2026-08-31, one week after
`adr/0027`) built `ago-faq` with:

- **no `tenants` table** — it scopes by the chat `SiteId` directly, as its own value type;
- **no `operators` and no `roles`** — its only two tables are `FaqModuleTask` and `KnowledgeBase`;
- console screens **inside `ago-console`** (`FaqModulePage`), gated by chat's own `usePermissions()`,
  talking to its own API origin through `config.faqApiBaseUrl`, and absent when that is unconfigured.

So the shape this stage wants is not a proposal. **It is the shipped pattern, and AGO Calendar is the
outlier** — it was built first and never caught up.

## The decision to record

**Domains stay apart. Tenancy and identity unify.**

`adr/0027` made two decisions in one document, and only one of them should survive re-weighing:

- **Separate domains — kept.** `Worker`, `Event`, `Service`, `WorkingHoursRule` are concepts
  `Ago.Chat.Domain` has no reason to grow. Merging them produces one bounded context that cannot
  later be split, and nothing in the product flow requires it. `adr/0027` was right here.
- **Separate tenancy, identity, role catalogues and consoles — reversed.** Every symptom comes from
  this half: two `tenants`, two `operators`, two role vocabularies, two SPAs, two Keycloak clients.

**Variant A (one database, one bounded context) stays rejected**, for `adr/0027`'s own reason.
**The platform-hosted `Tenant` also stays rejected**, and the reason is worth writing down because it
is not the one `adr/0027` gave: `Ago.Platform.*` ships as NuGet packages (`adr/0012`), so a `Tenant`
there is a shared *type* with no shared *data* — nothing unifies at runtime. Unifying for real needs
either a schema both products read (which is Variant A) or a new deployable on every request path.
And rule 8 would push the data back into each product anyway, since a write decision cannot read
another service inside its own transaction.

## What the amendment must say in as many words

- The account is one row. Products scope by its id and do not mint their own tenancy.
- One person is one Keycloak subject and one row on the account side. A product holds no `operators`.
- One role catalogue, on the account side. The two permission vocabularies are **disjoint by prefix**
  (`booking:*`/`calendar:*`/`customer:*` against `conversation:*`/`site:*`), so one catalogue carries
  both with no renaming — the fact that makes this cheap.
- Products keep their own domain schema, their own repository, their own API origin.
- **A product may still be provisioned standalone.** Its tenancy row *equals* the account id rather
  than being replaced by it — see `22-03`, and `20-27`'s provisioner stays for exactly this.
- `adr/0027`'s Consequences section is where the retained half is restated; the reversed half is
  superseded rather than deleted, with the date and the reason.

**And one consequence outside `adr/0027` entirely.** `adr/0091` settled hostnames as *"the bare
product name is that product's console, `-api` is its API"* — that scheme's premise is a console per
product, which this stage removes. It is superseded by `22-10`: **a hostname is either the console
(`office.`), one product's API (`<product>-api.`), or a thing that is not ours to name.** Bare product
names disappear. Recorded here because a naming rule nobody amends is a naming rule the next session
follows.

## Outcome

`adr/0093`, accepted 2026-09-03.

The decisive evidence was not in the calendar. **`ago-faq` already implements the target shape** —
`19-03`, 2026-08-31, one week after `adr/0027`, with no `tenants`, no `operators` and no `roles`,
scoping by the chat `SiteId` and gating its console screens on chat's own `usePermissions()`. So the
project had been running two contradictory patterns for ten days with no record of either winning, and
this ADR is as much a recording of that as a decision.

**The platform-hosted `Tenant` the author proposed was rejected on a mechanical ground, not a purity
one**, and that is the part worth re-reading: `Ago.Platform.*` ships as NuGet packages (`adr/0012`),
so a `Tenant` there is a shared *type* with no shared *data* — nothing unifies at runtime. Real
unification needs a schema both products read (Variant A, already rejected) or a new deployable on
every request path, and rule 8 pushes the data back into each product either way.

**Renaming `Site` to `Account` was deferred rather than rejected.** `Site` is already an account in
substance, but the rename touches `SiteId` in 458 of `ago-chat`'s 1067 files and buys nothing this
decision needs. Recorded in the ADR's Alternatives, where the next person to be tempted will find it.
